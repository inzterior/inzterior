Most people choosing an interior design studio in Johor Bahru start with the portfolio. Fair enough — it's the fun part, and it's the part every studio makes easy to look at. But a good portfolio tells you almost nothing about whether the studio will still be answering your calls three months into the project. That's a paperwork question, not a taste question, and it's the one most homeowners never think to ask until something's gone wrong.

This checklist is the one I'd want a family member to use before hiring anyone — including us. Everything on it is something you can actually verify yourself, independently of whatever the studio tells you.

## Start with the paperwork, not the portfolio

Before you look at a single mood board, find out if the studio is a real, registered business. In Malaysia, that means a Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM) registration number you can look up yourself — not just a business name on a Facebook page. A studio with nothing to hide will hand this over without hesitation, usually before you even ask.

This isn't about assuming bad faith. Plenty of genuinely skilled people work informally. But a studio with a real registered entity and a real studio address has something to lose by cutting corners on your project — a Facebook page and a WhatsApp number don't carry the same weight.

## Ask how payments are structured before you ask about price

Price is the question everyone leads with, and it's the wrong one to lead with. The structure of the payments matters more than the total, because it determines how much risk you're carrying at any given point in the project.

Be specific: ask what triggers each payment. A studio that can answer with real milestones — design sign-off, materials ordered, carpentry started, on-site installation, handover — is telling you payments follow verified progress. A studio that answers with "50% deposit, 50% on completion" is telling you very little, because that first 50% isn't tied to anything you can check. We've written more about what these red flags look like in practice in [how to spot a deposit-and-disappear contractor](/articles/spot-deposit-disappear-contractor-malaysia).

## Get the scope in writing before you get a price

A quotation with a total at the bottom is not a scope of work. Ask to see, in writing, what materials are actually specified — brand, spec, quantity where it matters — and what's explicitly excluded. Vague line items like "carpentry works" or "electrical as required" are where disputes are born, because there's nothing concrete to hold anyone to when something's missing later.

If a studio is reluctant to put this in writing before you commit, that reluctance is itself the answer. We go into more detail on what a complete contract should cover in [what should actually be in your interior design contract](/articles/interior-design-contract-guide-malaysia).

## Ask what happens after handover, not just during the project

A studio's real commitment to quality shows up after the keys are handed over, not during the sales pitch. Ask specifically: is there a defects warranty period, what does it cover, and what's the expected response time if something needs fixing? A studio that goes quiet after final payment was never really committed to the outcome — only to closing the sale. See our breakdown of [what a fair renovation warranty should cover](/articles/renovation-warranty-malaysia-guide) for what to expect.

## Verify past work in person, not just on Instagram

Ask to see a completed or in-progress project, ideally in person, or at minimum speak briefly to a past client. A studio that's actually done the work you're describing should be able to point you to something real — not just a curated feed of images that could be stock photography or someone else's portfolio.

This matters more in Johor Bahru specifically than it might elsewhere, simply because the market is growing fast enough that new "studios" appear constantly, and not all of them have delivered anything yet. Verified, in-person proof is the fastest way to tell the difference. If you're also deciding between a full design studio and a contractor-only route, our guide on [interior designer vs. contractor](/articles/interior-designer-vs-contractor-malaysia) walks through that decision.

## The checklist, condensed

- Real SSM registration and a verifiable studio address
- Payments tied to specific, checkable milestones — never one large sum upfront
- A written scope of work, with materials specified, before you sign anything
- A clearly stated defects warranty period with a real response-time commitment
- Verifiable past work you can see in person, not just curated photos
