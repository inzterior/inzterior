"Book a Consultation" is the button on nearly every page of our site, and it's usually the first thing a prospective client actually clicks — often before they've decided whether they even want a full renovation. That's fine. It's meant to be a low-commitment first step. But I've noticed people hesitate right before clicking it, usually because they don't actually know what happens next, or whether it's going to cost them anything. It's a fair thing to want cleared up before you hand over your contact details, so here's the honest walkthrough.

## Two things called "consultation" — and they're not the same

This is the part that causes the most confusion, so let's separate it clearly. We offer two different things that both get called a "consultation":

The **discovery consultation** is free, and it's the standard first step for anyone considering a full project with us — a renovation, a new-build fit-out, a full design-and-styling engagement. There's no obligation attached, and no pressure to sign anything at the end of it.

The **design consultation** is a separate, paid service, aimed at homeowners who want expert design direction or floor plan advice but plan to manage the actual build themselves — you're not hiring us to run the project, just to point it in the right direction. If that's closer to what you're after, our [Estimate page](/estimate) has it listed as its own scope option, separate from a full renovation.

If you're not sure which one fits you yet, start with the free discovery consultation — that conversation itself will usually make it obvious which path makes sense.

## What actually happens in a discovery consultation

We walk the space if it's an existing property, or review your floor plan if it isn't built or accessible yet. From there it's a conversation, not a pitch: your budget range, your timeline, and — the part we spend the most time on — how you actually live in the space day to day. Storage habits, who uses which room and when, whether you work from home, whether humidity or heat is a recurring problem in your current place. Good design starts from how a space gets used, not from how it photographs, so this is genuinely the most useful part of the whole conversation for us, not a formality before we show you a mood board.

Nothing gets signed at this stage. You walk away with a clearer sense of scope, a realistic ballpark, and — if it makes sense to go further — an outline of what a concept and proposal would involve. If you'd like a rough number before you even book the call, our [instant estimate calculator](/estimate) gives you a preliminary range going in, though your exact price only gets documented once we've actually scoped your project together.

**Worth bringing, if you have it:** a floor plan or unit layout, a rough budget range in mind, and photos of anything you're already drawn to or actively trying to avoid. None of it is required — plenty of people show up with nothing but a general sense that their current space isn't working for them, and that's a perfectly fine place to start.

## What actually happens in a paid design consultation

This one is narrower by design. You're paying for our design judgment — layout advice, material and finish direction, a floor plan you can hand to your own contractor — not for us to manage or execute the build. It suits homeowners who are confident running the construction side themselves but want a second, expert opinion on the design before they commit to a layout.

Because this is a standalone paid service rather than the first step of a larger engagement, the exact scope and fee get confirmed directly with us before booking — we'd rather tell you a real number in conversation than publish a figure online that doesn't account for your specific space. [Get in touch](/contact) and we can tell you what it looks like for your project.

## What happens after

If a discovery consultation leads somewhere, the next stage is a concept and proposal — mood boards, layout options, and an itemised quote, still nothing signed. Nothing beyond that point starts without a written contract covering scope, materials, and timeline; we've written about what a proper contract should actually contain in [what should actually be in your interior design contract](/articles/interior-design-contract-guide-malaysia), which is worth a read regardless of who you end up working with.

## How soon you'll hear back

We typically reply within 1–2 business days of a consultation request. If it's been longer than that and you haven't heard from us, something's gone wrong on our end — follow up, don't assume you fell through the cracks.

## Closing

The short version: the first conversation is free, there's no pressure attached to it, and its whole purpose is to figure out — together — whether a fuller engagement even makes sense for your space. If you've got a project in mind, [book a consultation](/contact) and we'll take it from there.
