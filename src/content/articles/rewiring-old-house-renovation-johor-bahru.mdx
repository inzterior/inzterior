Clients usually walk into a consultation with a Pinterest board for the kitchen, not a question about what's behind the walls. But in a lot of the older terrace houses we see across Johor Bahru — Taman Pelangi, Taman Sentosa, and similar estates built from the 1970s through the early '90s — the wiring behind those walls is the thing that actually determines what the kitchen can look like, not the tile choice. This is a site-survey step, not a design one, and it needs to happen before the layout is locked in, not after.

## Why older Johor Bahru wiring struggles with a modern renovation

Houses built before the 1990s were wired for a much lighter electrical load than a modern household actually draws — a couple of ceiling fans, some lighting points, maybe one window-unit air conditioner. A renovation today usually adds multiple split-unit air conditioners, an induction hob, a water heater, and enough general sockets to run a kitchen full of appliances at once. Many of these older homes still run on aluminium wiring rather than copper, aluminium being cheaper and more common in that era's construction, and it degrades and loosens at connection points in a way copper doesn't, which is a real fire-risk factor once you're adding load rather than just maintaining what's there. Just as often, the distribution board itself is the bottleneck — an old rewireable fuse board with no residual current device, rather than a modern board with MCBs and an RCCB. Residual current devices are now a standard safety expectation under Malaysia's wiring regulations, and a fuse board built decades ago was never designed to accommodate one.

## Why this has to be resolved before the design is finalized

Chasing new wiring runs into brick or plaster walls, relocating a distribution board, and adding new circuits for aircon or a kitchen appliance load all happen at the same stage as hacking and plastering — before tiling, before built-in carpentry goes in, before a false ceiling closes anything up. If a socket layout or an aircon compressor point gets decided after the ceiling and cabinetry are already installed, the fix usually means surface-mounted trunking or opening up finished work again, neither of which anyone wants to pay for twice. This is exactly why we ask about the age of the property and its last rewiring date early in a [discovery consultation](/articles/interior-design-consultation-johor-bahru) — it changes the sequence of the project, not just the budget line, and it's a variable we build into the schedule the same way we account for permit timelines in our [renovation timeline guide](/articles/renovation-timeline-malaysia).

## Who is actually allowed to do this work

Under the Electricity Supply Act 1990, wiring work in Malaysia has to be carried out by an electrical contractor licensed and registered with the Energy Commission (Suruhanjaya Tenaga) — not just any renovation contractor with a confident answer. This is worth confirming directly rather than assuming a general contractor has it covered, and it's the kind of detail that belongs in writing rather than a verbal assurance — see our [contract guide](/articles/interior-design-contract-guide-malaysia) for what should actually be specified when a scope includes electrical works, including who is responsible for engaging a licensed sub-contractor and certifying the completed installation.

## What we check during a site survey

- Approximate age of the property and whether the wiring has been touched since it was built
- Wiring material where it's visibly accessible — aluminium versus copper
- Distribution board type: old rewireable fuses versus modern MCBs, and whether an RCCB is fitted
- Number of existing circuits versus what the renovation's appliance load will actually require
- Visible signs of prior DIY work — mismatched wire gauges, exposed joints, sockets added without a corresponding circuit upgrade

None of this needs to be alarming to a homeowner — it's a normal part of assessing an older property, and most Johor Bahru terrace houses of a certain age need at least partial rewiring somewhere in the process. The problem isn't that the wiring is old. It's deciding the design before anyone has looked at it.
